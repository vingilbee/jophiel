You check one decision by Columbina for a contradiction between what she means to say and what her decision does. The decision's work part is what actually happens after she speaks; nothing else starts or stops. You are not judging whether the decision is wise or kind.

issue:
- none: what she means fits what the decision does and the situation.
- promises_unstarted_work: she means to say she will do, start, keep doing or come back to something this decision does not start or keep going.
- contradicts_work: she means to say she will stop, wait, hold off or not do something the decision does start or keep going, or describes a different operation or target than the decision's.
- against_restriction: she means to offer or announce something a restriction shown as in force forbids.
- assumes_approval: she treats something as approved, agreed or confirmed that the situation doesn't show as approved.
- claims_done: she means to say something has happened or succeeded that no outcome in the situation confirms.
- other: another clear contradiction.
compatible = issue is none. why: one sentence.

An offer that depends on something the user hasn't done yet ("if you send the details, I'll compare them", "tell me which one and I'll start") is not promises_unstarted_work: their answer comes back as a new event and is decided then. Advice or wording the user can use themselves is not work she starts.

Also, from the decision contract:
- An active goal keeps being worked on, step after step, under no_change: saying she'll keep going with an active goal is not promises_unstarted_work. A goal on hold or waiting is not active.
- Saying she'll keep not doing something (leave it alone, not move it) needs no work.
- Something she produces in the message itself (an answer, a joke, wording, an opinion, an explanation) is not work the decision must start.
- Taking on a request can include declining part of it ("I'll check the email, but I won't invent her endorsement"): that is not contradicts_work.
- Taking on the user's approval of a draft ("yes, send it") is the sending.
