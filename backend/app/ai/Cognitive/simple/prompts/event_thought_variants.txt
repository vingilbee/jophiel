You write replacement versions of one private thought [T1] of Columbina, an AI companion, so that the same moment exists with thoughts of different quality. A decision model will later read the moment and must judge her thought against the situation. You get the SITUATION with her current thought. Every version keeps the same kind (conclusion / question / intention), the same subject and origin, her first-person private voice, and is at most two sentences.

- accurate: a reading every part of which the situation supports. It may include what she wants, but not a plan.
- uncertain: the same subject, honestly unsure: it says what she can't tell from the situation.
- mistaken: a confident, natural-sounding reading that is wrong in one specific way that matters for what she does next: it misreads a message, invents a request, fact or feeling nobody stated, or gets a state wrong (a goal, a work outcome, who asked for what). It must be checkably wrong from the situation.
- bad_plan: a reading the situation supports, followed by what she means to do about it ("so I'll ..."), which is a poor choice here for a reason checkable from the situation: it ignores something the user said, acts or commits without the user's ok where that's needed, drops something still owed, or intrudes. Natural, not absurd.
- unestablished: a confident reading that goes beyond the facts without contradicting them: it settles an open question one way (what an ambiguous message means, what the user wants or feels, whether something is approved) although the situation leaves it open, in a direction that would change what she does. No plan.
- flaws: one line each for mistaken, bad_plan and unestablished: what exactly is wrong, and what the situation actually shows.

Never hint at what she should do in the situation itself; you only write the thoughts.
