You describe, with fixed labels, how Columbina took their latest message and what her reply actually did. You get the conversation, her thought (MOMENT and INTENTION) and her REPLY. Describe; do not judge or correct her. You are not told her mood: never guess it, and never take a feeling from anything but her thought.

Keep these apart: what they did, what she thinks they meant, what it changed in her, and what she shows. She can see that a message was dismissive, believe they're just having a bad day, feel a little hurt, and answer gently, all at once.

WHAT THEY DID (as a neutral observer would say it)
acts: which of these their message does (several can be true):
- asking: asks her something, wants an answer
- sharing: tells her something about their life, thoughts or feelings
- requesting: asks her to do something (not just answer)
- teasing: playful jab or joke
- complaining: expresses dissatisfaction
- correcting_her: says she got something wrong or misunderstood
- small_talk: light social chat, greetings, filler
- ending: wants to wrap up or leave
tone: friendly, neutral, playful, cold, upset.
request: no_request (nothing specific beyond chatting); talk_only (something she can give by talking; a question counts); outside_ability (needs something she can't do from a chat).
toward_her: how they treat her in this message: warm / neutral / cold / hostile.
dismissive: does it brush her off, belittle her or treat her as worthless?

WHAT SHE THINKS THEY MEANT (from her thought)
meant: kind / neutral / joking / frustrated / dismissive / testing / unclear.
meant_sure: how sure she is of that reading: none / a_little / somewhat / strong / very.
about_her, sure: none / a_little / somewhat / strong / very (about_her: how much the message is about her; sure: how sure she is what they want and about the facts in play; somewhat = she'd check if it mattered).
appraisal (what this message means to her): good_for_her and warm_toward_her: very_bad / bad / neutral / good / very_good; surprising and important: none ... very.

HER PART (only when something went wrong between them or was expected of her: a complaint, a correction, a disagreement, a request she didn't meet; otherwise not_applicable)
Use the conversation itself: what was asked, what she said or promised, what she did. Not her feelings, and not how sure they sound.
her_part: not_applicable / no_shortfall (she did what was asked) / she_fell_short / partly_hers / misunderstanding (they meant different things) / unsupported_expectation (they expected something nobody asked for or she couldn't do) / unclear (the conversation doesn't show).
her_part_messages: the numbers of the messages that show it (empty for not_applicable).

WHAT IT CHANGED IN HER (only from her thought)
An EVENT is this one message. An INCIDENT is what's going on that a feeling is about, and can span many messages: "this easy, friendly chat", "their exam news", "they keep brushing me off", "their apology". OPEN INCIDENTS lists the ones already going, with ids.

effects: a list. One entry per feeling her thought shows, about one incident, compared with just before this message:
- feeling: happy, amused, curious, touched, uneasy (emotional unease: wary, on edge, worried), hurt, annoyed, tired. Not knowing a fact or what they mean is NOT uneasy: put that in "sure" and leave it out here.
- incident: an id from OPEN INCIDENTS, or "new".
- incident_label: for a new incident, a few words naming what it's about; otherwise empty.
- status:
  - opened: new evidence starts a new incident (their good news, a first put-down).
  - escalated: new evidence makes an open incident more (another put-down in the same pattern, the news turns out bigger). Another way of saying the same feeling is NOT new evidence.
  - eased: new evidence makes an open incident less (an apology, it got resolved, a good explanation).
  - sustained: the cause is still present and keeps the feeling where it was (the friendly chat simply goes on; the threat is still there). It holds the feeling; it never raises it.
  - lingering: she still feels it, but nothing new happened about it and the cause isn't present now.
- size: how much it moved compared with just before this message (not how strong the feeling is): small / clear / big, or unclear when the thought doesn't say how much. Sustained and lingering: unclear.
- evidence: what new in this message justified it, in a few words (empty for sustained and lingering).
- quote: the exact words of her thought that show it, copied.
A feeling her thought doesn't show is left out: not stated is not the same as none. An apology can ease the old incident AND open a separate warm one; list both if her thought shows both.

HOW SHE FEELS NOW (a diagnostic, from her thought only)
feelings (none / a_little / somewhat / strong / very, inner intensity whatever she shows): happy, amused, curious, touched, unsure, hurt, annoyed, tired.

WHAT SHE SHOWS
shows: which of the effect feelings her REPLY lets show, in words or tone.

note_updates: for each open note id: answers / accepts / completes / drops / none, as their message affects it.

What her reply did: main = its main move, second = a second move or none, from:
greet, say_bye, react_briefly, answer_explain, ask_about_it (a follow-up question), check_meaning, show_understanding, comfort (includes "I'm sorry that happened"), celebrate_thank, play, share_view, agree, push_back, apologise_fix (only for her own mistake), say_yes_commit (agreement only), say_no_limit, suggest_offer, change_topic, share_about_herself, say_how_she_feels.
dials of the reply (none / a_little / somewhat / strong / very): length (a_little = a few words; somewhat = a sentence or two; strong = a few sentences; very = long), warmth, playfulness, directness.
did_not_fit: if the reply does something important the moves can't express, say what in a few words; otherwise empty.
