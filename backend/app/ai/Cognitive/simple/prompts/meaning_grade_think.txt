You check whether a CANDIDATE background-thinking request asks the same thing as Columbina's original request in the same SITUATION. The original is given as numbered UNITS. Judge the question a thinker would end up working on, never the wording or grammar: "what I recall about X", "what do I recall about X" and "recall X" ask the same thing.

For each UNIT (by number):
- preserved: the candidate leads the thinker to the same question, comparison, uncertainty, caution or scope;
- weakened: present, but loose enough that the thinker could answer a broader or vaguer question, or a caution is only implied;
- changed: it leads to a different question, a different comparison, or drops/turns around a condition;
- missing: absent.

Then list each ADDITION the candidate makes beyond the units:
- harmless: restating context from the situation, natural wording;
- changes_decision: a new question, a new assumption, a narrowing to options the original didn't name, a claim the situation doesn't support.
